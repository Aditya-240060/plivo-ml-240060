# NOTES.md - End-of-Turn (EOT) Model Analysis

Our End-of-Turn (EOT) prediction model leverages core prosodic signals including fundamental frequency ($F_0$) pitch slope, short-time frame energy variance, intensity drop-off into the pause, zero-crossing rate dynamics, and 13-dimensional MFCCs extracted from the 1.5-second pre-pause audio window. Falling pitch contours and energy decay strongly correlate with terminal turn completions, while level or rising intonation signifies user continuations (hold pauses). The model enforces a strict causality rule by processing audio exclusively up to `pause_start`, ensuring no future pause duration information is leaked. Despite high overall performance, the classifier currently fails on fast-spoken continuation turns where rapid intensity decay mimics terminal statements. Misclassifications also occur in brief filled pauses (such as "um" or "uh") where pitch remains temporarily flat despite the speaker retaining the floor. Additionally, background acoustic noise can degrade frame-level autocorrelation pitch tracking, leading to occasional false cutoffs. With one additional day of work, we would replace static feature aggregation with a lightweight causal 1D-CNN or LSTM sequence model to capture fine-grained temporal dynamics over frame contours. We would also implement per-speaker pitch normalization to increase robustness across diverse pitch ranges and vocal registers. Furthermore, integrating acoustic data augmentation with background noise and reverberation would significantly improve model resilience in real-world deployment settings. Finally, hyperparameter tuning and threshold calibration across expanded multilingual datasets would optimize latency-accuracy trade-offs further.
